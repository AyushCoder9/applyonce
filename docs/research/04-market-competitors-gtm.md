# ApplyOnce — Market, Competitors, Business Model & GTM Research
*Compiled 2026-09-02. All figures cited to source; treat anything not linked as directional/general-knowledge framing rather than a fresh statistic.*

## A. Global analogs

**Singapore — Singpass + Myinfo (closest analog).** Singpass has ~97% adoption among citizens/PRs, 5M users (4.2M on the app), and >41M transactions/month across 2,700+ services and 800+ agencies ([tech.gov.sg](https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/singpass/), [OECD-OPSI](https://oecd-opsi.org/innovations/singpass/)). **Myinfo** is the pre-fill layer sitting on top: >200,000 transactions/day across 800+ onboarded digital services, cutting application time by up to 80% and lifting business approval rates ~15% through better data quality ([Adnovum](https://www.adnovum.com/blog/what-is-singpass-myinfo-and-how-does-it-enhance-your-services)). The **consent screen is dynamically generated from the API scope the requester asks for** — a business specifies which data items (name, mobile, income, address, etc.) it needs, Myinfo renders exactly that list for the user to approve or decline field-by-field, and a user can always opt to fill manually instead ([Singpass Partner Support](https://partnersupport.singpass.gov.sg/hc/en-sg/articles/32840739143321), [MyInfo API spec](https://public.cloud.myinfo.gov.sg/myinfo/api/myinfo-kyc-v3.1.0.html)). **Myinfo Business** does the same for corporates — auto-populating grant and licensing forms with corporate profile, ownership, shareholders and financials ([Getting Started](https://www.developer.tech.gov.sg/products/categories/digital-identity/myinfo/getting-started), [MyInfo Business spec](https://public.cloud.myinfo.gov.sg/myinfobiz/myinfo-biz-specs-v2.0.html)). **Business model:** freemium — first 5,000 standard transactions/month per business (UEN) are free, then metered, with a pricier "Myinfo Plus" tier for sensitive scopes; no onboarding fee ([Singpass Pricing FAQ](https://partnersupport.singpass.gov.sg/hc/en-sg/articles/34880999253145)) — the single best pricing template for ApplyOnce's B2B2C fee.

**Estonia — X-Road.** Public infrastructure, not a business: a decentralized "data resides where created" exchange where every institution keeps its own database and X-Road brokers signed, encrypted, logged queries between them; citizens see who accessed their data via the State Portal, and it's now co-run by Estonia, Finland and Iceland through the non-profit NIIS ([e-Estonia](https://e-estonia.com/service/interoperability-services/x-road/), [GovInsider](https://govinsider.asia/intl-en/article/estonias-x-road-data-exchange-in-the-worlds-most-digital-society)). Lesson: pure-plumbing DPI isn't monetizable by a private player — value capture happens one layer up, in the application experience ApplyOnce builds.

**EU Digital Identity Wallet (EUDI/eIDAS 2.0).** Every member state must offer at least one wallet by December 2026; fewer than a third currently meet readiness benchmarks. Italy's IT-Wallet has been in public beta since October 2024 (driving licence, health card, EHIC); Poland's mObywatel is already in production; Denmark's AltID went live June 2026; Germany and Sweden entered beta in early 2026. From late 2027, large platforms and regulated sectors (banking, healthcare, telecom) must accept the wallet as a primary auth method ([eIDeasy, Aug 2026](https://www.eideasy.com/blog/eu-digital-identity-wallets-august-2026), [Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/eudi-wallet-2026-deadline-rollout-eic-2026)). Lesson: even a rich 27-country bloc needs 4+ years and still isn't uniformly live — exactly the window a private "verify once" layer can fill in India.

**UK — GOV.UK One Login.** 11M+ verified users, 80+ services onboarded; HMRC began onboarding new users in February 2026; a digital Veteran Card shipped with 15,000+ downloads, and an mDL is being tested for 2026; by end-2027 One Login becomes the *only* way into central government services ([THINK Digital Partners](https://www.thinkdigitalpartners.com/news/2025/10/06/government-eyes-full-adoption-of-gov-uk-one-login-by-2027/), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/01/20/iphone-drivers-licenses-here-are-all-the-states-working-on-support/)). The UK doing what MeriPehchaan attempts in India — consolidating dozens of legacy department logins into one identity.

**Australia — Digital ID Act 2024.** Commenced 1 December 2024, creating an accredited multi-provider framework (AGDIS); myGovID was renamed **myID** to avoid confusion with myGov; Services Australia runs the ID exchange, and *private* entities can be accredited as identity/credential providers under law ([Digital ID System](https://www.digitalidsystem.gov.au/what-is-digital-id/digital-id-act-2024), [Gilbert+Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/digital-id-act-2024)). This is the clearest global precedent for what ApplyOnce ultimately wants in India — a legislated accreditation path for private identity/attribute providers — but India has no equivalent Digital ID Act yet, so ApplyOnce must build on existing rails (Aadhaar Act, DPDP Act, AA framework) rather than wait for one.

**US — ID.me vs Login.gov.** Login.gov is government-run, no biometrics, no data-selling. ID.me is a **private company** doing the same job commercially: 156M+ users, live at 21 federal agencies, 50 state agencies and 70+ healthcare organizations, $130M revenue (2023) ([Wikipedia/ID.me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ID.me), [ID.me Government](https://www.id.me/government)). ID.me is the single strongest existing proof that a *private* company can become quasi-government identity infrastructure at scale and get paid for it — a direct precedent for ApplyOnce's B2G ambitions.

**Yoti.** Free, reusable Digital ID wallet (23M+ installs) built so Yoti itself cannot monetize or access user data — it charges *businesses* to receive verified attributes ("verify once, use many times"), and is UK DIATF-certified ([Yoti](https://www.yoti.com/business/digital-id/), [nhimg.org](https://nhimg.org/articles/privacy-preserving-digital-id-raises-the-bar-for-reusable-identity/)). Architecturally closest of all the Western players to what ApplyOnce should be: consumer-free, B2B-paid, privacy-by-design.

**Persona.** Usage-based B2B pricing: $250/month "Essential" plan plus $0.10–$0.45 per verification check across 200+ countries, covering KYC/KYB/AML/age/employee verification ([Persona pricing](https://withpersona.com/pricing), [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/persona)). Useful raw per-check pricing anchor for ApplyOnce's institution fee.

**Plaid — "connect once" model.** The direct financial analog to ApplyOnce's "verify once" idea: developers integrate once and pull data from 12,000+ institutions; Plaid hit $546M ARR in 2025 (+40% YoY, ~70% from transaction fees), reaching full-year EBITDA profitability the same year ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/plaid/)). Confirms that a connector/aggregator layer, monetized on usage by the *consuming* side (not the citizen), can become a large, profitable business — ApplyOnce's B2B2C model is structurally a Plaid-for-facts.

**Clear Secure.** Recurring biometric membership (CLEAR Plus) plus an expanding enterprise identity line (Atlas Certified, CLEAR1) reaching beyond airports into workplace/financial identity: 43.5M members, Q2 revenue $277.8M (+26.6% YoY), ~37% five-year CAGR ([ID Tech Wire](https://idtechwire.com/clear-reaches-43-5-million-members-as-q2-revenue-rises-27-percent/), [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/20/1107002/clear-airport-identity-management-biometrics-facial-recognition/)). A hybrid consumer-subscription + enterprise model can scale, but it needed a captive high-frequency wedge (airport security) first — ApplyOnce needs an equivalent.

**Common App (education).** The single most transferable model: **1,097 member colleges** and ~1.5M distinct first-year applicants in 2024-25 (+5% YoY) ([Common App blog](https://www.commonapp.org/blog/common-app-launches-2024-2025-application-season-over-1100-colleges-and-universities/)); the 2025-26 season logged a record **10,786,782 applications**, the largest in the platform's 50-year history ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/admissions/traditional-age/2026/03/13/common-app-data-shows-increase-applications)). It's a non-profit membership consortium — colleges pay tiered annual fees, not per-application fees — proving "fill once, send to many" works at national scale in education, the wedge CollegeDekho has copied in India (Section C) and one ApplyOnce should study for its own institution-fee design.

**Apply-with-LinkedIn / Indeed and autofill extensions.** LinkedIn/Indeed profile-based one-click apply, plus a whole cottage industry of third-party Chrome extensions (Autolin, FastApply, JobPilot, LazyApply — some with 10,000+ users) that scrape a resume/profile once and autofill *any* unintegrated Applicant Tracking System ([Fill Hero](https://fillhero.com/autofill/linkedin/), [JobPilot](https://www.jobpilotapp.com/chrome-extension)). This is a direct, already-proven validation of ApplyOnce's own "browser extension autofills legacy portals" mechanic — the pattern works and users already trust it for something as sensitive as job applications.

**Google/Apple Wallet mobile driving licences.** Apple Wallet mDL support reached only ~14–15 US states by 2026 (Arizona, California, Colorado, Georgia, Virginia, Arkansas, etc.), rolling out one state at a time over years, gated by REAL ID and TSA participation ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/18/apple-wallet-drivers-licenses-states-coming-soon/), [TSA](https://www.tsa.gov/digital-id/participating-states)). Even a wealthy, single-standard country is this slow — reinforcing why a private aggregation layer is needed to bridge fragmented, state-by-state rollouts, exactly the fragmentation ApplyOnce faces across Indian states.

**Bhutan NDI.** The world's first national-scale self-sovereign identity (SSI) system, live since October 2023, built on W3C Verifiable Credentials, recently migrated onto Ethereum (~800,000 citizens expected on the upgraded platform) ([Trust Over IP case study](https://trustoverip.org/wp-content/uploads/Case-Study-Bhutan-NDI-National-Digital-Identity-ToIP-Digital-Trust-Ecosystems-V1.0-2024-05-21.ext_.pdf), [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202410/bhutan-brings-first-national-digital-id-program-to-global-acceptance-network)). Not directly transferable at India's scale, but its wallet-held, consent-shared verifiable-credential design is a good technical reference for ApplyOnce's "structured facts" data model.

**Ukraine Diia.** The best-executed "everything in one national app" precedent: 23M+ users (~81% of the adult population), ~200 services and 33 digital documents, 59% of Ukrainians used e-government services in 2025 (48% via Diia specifically), 85% satisfaction ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/ukraine/press-releases/63-ukrainians-use-state-e-services-user-numbers-grow-third-year-row-survey), [Cabinet of Ministers](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/en/news/ponad-21-milion-ukraintsiv-korystuiutsia-diieiu-de-dostupni-24-dokumenty-ta-ponad-30-servisiv)). Diia is the direct "what if government builds this itself, well" scenario — the biggest strategic risk discussed in Section F.

## B. India landscape

**DigiLocker.** 67.63 crore (676.3M) users as of March 2026, up from 51.52 crore a year earlier — roughly 4x growth since 2022 — with over 950 crore (9.5B) documents issued, 2,131 issuers and 2,611 requesters (2025) ([dial.global](https://dial.global/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/The-DigiLocker-Story.pdf), [RTI Wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/digilocker-issuer-reality-india-2026)). Critically, DigiLocker is a **document locker and pull-based sharing rail**, not a structured-fact autofill engine — it hands over a certificate/PDF/XML, not pre-filled form fields. This is ApplyOnce's central wedge: DigiLocker solved "storage and retrieval," not "fill this specific form."

**UMANG.** 11.66 crore registered users and ~798 crore cumulative transactions over three years (597 crore by May 2025), spanning ~2,575 active services (880 central + 1,695 state, July 2026) ([ANI/Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/umang-records-over-11-crore-registered-users-798-crore-transactions-in-three-years-it-ministry/amp/)). UMANG is a services super-app/portal — again not an autofill layer.

**Entity Locker.** Launched by MeitY/NeGD around January 2025 as DigiLocker's business-facing sibling: 10GB encrypted storage, Aadhaar-authenticated access, integrated with MCA/GSTN/DGFT, aimed at MSME loan applications, vendor verification, FSSAI/GST/MCA filings ([informatics.nic.in](https://informatics.nic.in/files/websites/april-2025/entity-locker-a-game-changer-in-business-document-management.php)). Shows MeitY is actively extending the "locker" pattern into new domains fast — relevant to the "will government build this" risk below.

**MeriPehchaan (NSSO).** India's actual "single citizen profile" push: launched July 2022, unifying Jan Parichay, e-ApplyOnce and DigiLocker into one login (Aadhaar/PAN/DL + OTP MFA) giving access to 1,992+ digital services with one credential set ([Digital India](https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/janparichay-meri-pehchaan-the-national-single-sign-on/), [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/meripehchaan-national-sign-nsso-2025/)). This solves *authentication* fragmentation, not *form-filling* fragmentation — it does not pre-fill forms; it just lets you use one login across many portals. Directly answers the prompt's question: **no current Indian government product auto-fills arbitrary third-party (exam/college/hospital) forms today.**

**Account Aggregator (Sahamati/DEPA).** The closest domestic architecture to what ApplyOnce needs to emulate for consent-based data movement: 2.88 billion accounts enabled to share data (March/July 2026), 780+ financial institutions onboarded, 269M+ consents processed by September 2025, with linked accounts nearly doubling between September 2025 and March 2026 ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/media-article/indias-account-aggregator-framework-crosses-100-million-consents-in-three-years/), [CASParser](https://casparser.in/blog/state-of-account-aggregator-2026/)). AA proves India can run a working, RBI-recognized, consent-artefact-based, no-data-custody intermediary (a "Consent Manager" under DEPA) at billion-scale — ApplyOnce's consent ledger should be architected the same way rather than invent a new pattern.

**APAAR (One Nation One Student ID).** 26.35 crore verified IDs generated as of July 2026 against a target base of ~26 crore school students, voluntary, school-enrolled with parental consent, tied to the Academic Bank of Credits ([newkerala.com](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/2635-crore-verified-apaar-ids-generated-across-india-346.htm), [Insights on India](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2025/03/20/apaar-id/)). ApplyOnce should treat APAAR as a **source of verified education facts to ingest**, not a competitor to rebuild.

**ONDC (DPI pattern, not identity, but instructive).** 3.5 lakh+ sellers onboarded and 1.2 crore+ monthly transactions as of January 2025, built on the DPI pattern of government seeding an open protocol while private "network participants" build the apps and capture the margin ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2090097&reg=48&lang=2)). This is the template ApplyOnce should aim for on the institution side: become a trusted **network participant/gateway** on top of DigiLocker/AA/ABDM rails, not a rival rail.

**Bhashini.** National language-AI DPI: powers 800+ government websites, >15M inferences/day, 1,000+ pre-trained models across all 22 scheduled languages ([Elets eGov](https://egov.eletsonline.com/2026/03/bhashini-bridging-1-4-billion-citizens-to-services-and-opportunities-via-language-ai/)). Relevant as an off-the-shelf building block ApplyOnce can call for Hindi-friendly chat/help copy rather than build translation itself.

**eSanjeevani.** World's largest government telemedicine service: 43 crore (430M) consultations by November 2025, 220,000–230,000 onboarded providers, 57% women and 12% senior-citizen beneficiaries ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1344317/esanjeevani-number-of-patients-and-providers/), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41158553/)). Relevant to ApplyOnce's "health" fact category and hospital OPD-registration autofill use case.

**DigiYatra — the cautionary lesson.** Reports of airport staff enrolling passengers **without informed consent**, criticized publicly as running on "a defective model of consent" (TechPolicy.Press), with the Internet Freedom Foundation flagging third-party SDK data sharing, and the DigiYatra Foundation structured as a Section-8 company that sits partly outside RTI scrutiny ([The India Forum](https://www.theindiaforum.in/technology/digi-yatra-service-or-surveillance), [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/digi-yatra-and-the-defect-in-the-idea-of-consent/), [Panoptic](https://panoptic.in/case-study/the-dangers-of-digiyatra-facial-recognition-enabled-paperless-air-travel-saveourprivacy)). This is a live, documented example of exactly the trust failure ApplyOnce must not repeat — every consent screen has to be genuinely optional, legible, and auditable, never nudged by an intermediary (a VLE, a college clerk, an airport staffer) on the citizen's behalf.

**DEPA & DPDP consent managers.** The DPDP Act 2023 creates a regulated intermediary role — the "Consent Manager," registered with the Data Protection Board and further specified by the DPDP Rules 2025; NITI Aayog's DEPA framework is the design blueprint (interoperable, purpose-limited "consent artefacts," no access to underlying data), and RBI's Account Aggregator model is the live working example ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/india-dpdpa-consent-managers/), [azb](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/consent-managers-under-indias-dpdp-act-and-dpdp-rules/)). ApplyOnce's consent ledger should be built DEPA-compliant from day one so formal registration later is certification, not a rebuild.

**State single-window and "family ID" systems.** Maharashtra's **Aaple Sarkar** offers 536 services through a single citizen window ([aaplesarkar.mahaonline.gov.in](https://aaplesarkar.mahaonline.gov.in/en)); Karnataka's **Seva Sindhu** covers certificates (income, caste), welfare schemes and bus passes but is state-siloed ([sevaasindhukarnataka.com](https://sevaasindhukarnataka.com/across-india/)). The standout is **Haryana's Parivar Pehchan Patra (PPP)** — a verified, centralized family profile (names, ages, relationships, income, demographics) under one family ID that gateways 650+ schemes and **actually auto-enrolls and auto-updates eligibility** for pensions, scholarships and subsidies without re-application ([globaldrishti.in](https://globaldrishti.in/haryana-parivar-pehchan-patra-2026-family-id-apply/), [haryanafamilyid.com](https://haryanafamilyid.com/schemes/)). **This is the only mechanism found in India today that genuinely auto-fills/auto-applies on a citizen's behalf** — but it is state-government-only, welfare-scoped, and unusable for private exam/college/KYC forms. Rajasthan's Jan Aadhaar, UP's e-Sathi, and Andhra Pradesh's household-survey-driven family ID push follow a broadly similar logic, though current statistics for these were not independently verified here. **Net finding:** the family-ID pattern validates ApplyOnce's "family graph + one-tap apply" vision but exists only within single-state welfare stacks, leaving cross-institution exam/college/KYC use wide open.

## C. Private competitors and adjacents

**KYC/verification API infrastructure (Signzy, HyperVerge, IDfy, Karza/Perfios, Digio, Setu, Zoop, Surepass, Gridlines, Decentro, Bureau).** These firms power the backend of most Indian neobanks, brokers and lenders: Signzy has raised $38.7M across 7 rounds; IDfy raised $24.3M (Feb 2026) with 70M+ verifications for 600+ customers; Karza (under Perfios) is known for BFSI data depth; HyperVerge for AI document/biometric accuracy; Digio for legal e-sign; Setu, Surepass, Gridlines, Decentro and Zoop provide modular PAN/Aadhaar/GST/bank-account verification APIs, priced per enterprise contract ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/signzytechnologies/__FKmMW-C-ZqyWKlnCs2jeeZcdbBJFnHhjkB4fWlblLW4), [productgrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/insights/fintech/kyc-vendors-compared/), [Gridlines](https://gridlines.io/blogs/top-11-kyc-api-providers-in-india/)). **These are not really competitors** — they are point-verification vendors called once per transaction, with no persistent consumer-owned profile. ApplyOnce's correct posture is to **buy their APIs as suppliers** rather than rebuild verification primitives, differentiating on the consumer-owned, reusable, consent-ledgered layer none of them offer.

**CollegeDekho's Common Application Form (CAF).** India's largest existing "apply to multiple colleges with one form" product, covering information on 35,000+ colleges since 2015 ([CollegeDekho CAF](https://www.collegedekho.com/common-application-form), [LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/collegedekho)); Shiksha and Collegedunia run comparable aggregator/lead-generation models. **Their business model is lead-generation** — colleges pay for student leads/enquiries, not for verified, portable data — which is structurally the opposite of ApplyOnce's consent-first model and a point of real differentiation: ApplyOnce would give the *student* durable ownership and reuse of verified facts, not sell their contact details onward.

**Meritto (formerly NoPaperForms).** The admissions CRM operating system behind enquiry-to-enrollment for 1,200+ institutions across India, UAE and Southeast Asia, spanning K-12 schools, coaching institutes, study-abroad consultants and higher-ed ([Meritto](https://www.meritto.com/about-us/)). Given its footprint sits exactly at the point where colleges collect applicant data, **Meritto is a natural distribution/integration partner rather than a competitor** — a "Verified with ApplyOnce" widget inside Meritto's application forms would look very much like Myinfo plugging into a Singaporean business form.

**CSC/VLE network — the underrated channel.** 5.3–5.8 lakh operational Common Service Centres (534,000 in April 2025, rising past 582,000 by August 2025) run by 500,000+ Village Level Entrepreneurs, each typically covering ~6 villages, already delivering G2C/B2C/financial services and — informally — filling forms for citizens for a fee ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/common-services-centres-scheme-india/), [PIB on VLE AI training](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2145349&reg=48&lang=2)). This is a ready-made, government-endorsed **last-mile channel**: VLEs already have citizen trust and a commission-based business model for exactly this kind of task, making them a far cheaper acquisition channel than paid digital marketing for Bharat-market users.

**Leverage Edu / Yocket (adjacent, not competing).** Full-stack study-abroad platforms bundling university matching, test prep, loans, forex and accommodation; Leverage Edu posted FY26 revenue of ₹375 crore (+112% YoY) across 16 source countries ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/visa-crackdowns-are-blocking-students-study-abroad-dreams-so-indias-leverage-edu-is-rerouting-them/)). These operate in outbound international education, a different vertical from ApplyOnce's domestic exam/scholarship/KYC focus, though they illustrate the "assisted full-stack" alternative to ApplyOnce's self-serve model.

**Truecaller, CRED, Jupiter, PhonePe/Paytm KYC, Zerodha KYC.** These consumer apps run their own Aadhaar/PAN-based KYC flows to onboard users into their *own* walled garden — none of them expose a portable, reusable identity/fact-sharing layer other institutions can consume; Truecaller in particular remains focused on caller ID/spam-blocking rather than formal identity verification products. The gap ApplyOnce fills is precisely this: none of today's high-trust consumer apps let a user *export* their verified profile to a third party.

**Who is closest to "profile autofill for exams" today?** No one, cleanly. CollegeDekho/Shiksha solve multi-college applications but via lead-gen, not verified reusable data; Meritto solves the institution back-office but isn't consumer-facing; job-application autofill extensions (Section A) solve the mechanic but for résumés, not verified government facts; Haryana's PPP solves auto-enrollment but only for state welfare schemes. ApplyOnce's opportunity is the union of all four: verified facts (not resume text) + consumer ownership (not lead-gen) + consent ledger (not a CRM) + extension autofill for the long tail of legacy exam/scholarship/hospital portals that will never get a native API.

## D. Business model options and TAM/SAM/SOM

**Pricing benchmarks to anchor a B2B2C fee.** Singapore's Myinfo: free up to 5,000 transactions/month per business, then metered, with a premium tier for sensitive scopes ([Singpass Pricing FAQ](https://partnersupport.singpass.gov.sg/hc/en-sg/articles/34880999253145)). Persona: $250/month base + $0.10–$0.45 per verification ([Persona pricing](https://withpersona.com/pricing)). Setu's Account Aggregator integration reportedly charges a flat ₹10–₹25 per successful consent-based data fetch. India's own KYC software market was ~$45.9M in 2025, projected to reach $496.1M by 2034 (30.8% CAGR) — still tiny relative to the global $6.73B (2025) → $16.31B (2031) market, implying India is under-monetized and ripe for a domestic-first vertical player ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/e-kyc-market), [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/kyc-market)). A defensible starting price for ApplyOnce is **₹15–₹40 per verified application pushed to an institution**, undercutting a manual document-verification clerk's cost while sitting comfortably inside these global per-check benchmarks.

**Volume anchors for TAM/SAM.** JEE Main Session 1 2026 drew ~14.5 lakh registrations and Session 2 ~11.1 lakh, combined unique candidates ~15–17 lakh for the cycle ([pw.live](https://www.pw.live/iit-jee/exams/jee-mains-2026-results-statistics), [smartachievers.online](https://smartachievers.online/jee-main-2026-registration-numbers-latest-updates)); SSC CGL 2025 drew 28.15 lakh applicants for 14,582 posts, and SSC CHSL 2025 drew 3 million+ for 3,131 posts ([careerpower.in](https://www.careerpower.in/blog/28-lakhs-candidates-applied-for-ssc-cgl-2025-exam), [adda247](https://www.adda247.com/exams/ssc/ssc-chsl-received-over-3-million-applications-for-3131-posts/)) — just two of SSC's dozen-plus annual exams, consistent with the commonly cited ~50 lakh/year SSC total. NEET UG (~24 lakh) and CUET UG (~13–15 lakh) are widely reported NTA-scale figures, though not freshly re-verified in this pass. On welfare, **NSP** disbursed ₹8,000 crore to **over 2 crore students** in FY2024-25 across 140+ merged schemes, a 95% jump in applications via Digital India scholarship schemes ([vajiramandravi.com](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/national-scholarship-portal-nsp/)). On financial data, the Account Aggregator network spans **2.88 billion linked accounts** and **780+ institutions**, sizing how large a consent-based "verified data pull" market can get ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/media-article/indias-account-aggregator-framework-crosses-100-million-consents-in-three-years/)).

Putting this together: **TAM** ≈ every application requiring identity/eligibility proof in India per year — competitive exams (SSC ~50L, JEE ~15L, NEET ~24L, CUET ~13-15L, state PSCs, railway/banking recruitment — collectively well over 1.5–2 crore applications/year), scholarships (NSP ~2 crore students/year), college admissions (Common-App-style aggregators already process tens of lakhs of applications), and KYC events across fintech/telecom (hundreds of millions of Aadhaar e-KYC transactions/year, given 2.21 billion Aadhaar authentications in August 2025 alone). **SAM** is the subset reachable through legacy-portal autofill and institution partnerships in the first 2-3 years — realistically scholarships, SSC/state-PSC-style recruitment exams, and Meritto-powered private colleges, on the order of 3–5 crore applications/year. **SOM** for year one is a few lakh verified profiles and tens of thousands of completed autofilled applications across 2-3 pilot exam bodies/institutions and one CSC-channel district — a deliberately narrow beachhead (see Section G).

**Business model menu, ranked by near-term viability:**
1. **B2B2C per-verified-application fee** (institutions/exam bodies/scholarship portals pay ₹15–40 per application submitted "with ApplyOnce," modeled on Myinfo/Persona/Setu benchmarks above) — the primary model.
2. **CSC/VLE channel commission** — VLEs earn a small commission (comparable to existing CSC service commissions) for onboarding citizens and creating verified profiles, turning India's 500,000+ VLE network into a distribution force rather than a competitor.
3. **B2C premium** — family document vault, faster re-verification, priority support (Yoti/Clear-style optional consumer upsell); secondary revenue, not the core engine early on.
4. **Government/DPI partnership or tender** — an Entity-Locker/API-Setu-style MoU with a state government's scholarship or single-window portal, similar in spirit to how ONDC onboards private network participants onto a government-seeded protocol.
5. **Coaching-institute and university-CRM (Meritto) partnerships** — white-labeled "Verified Apply" integration inside existing admissions software, revenue-shared rather than competing for the same institution relationship.

## E. Regulatory sequencing and GTM enablers

**Day 1 buildable:** synthetic provider flows, manual non-Aadhaar document upload, ApplyOnce-hosted forms, partner APIs and user-controlled browser autofill. DigiLocker still requires Requester approval. Real Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC or Aadhaar App VC verification requires OVSE registration under UIDAI's December 2025 amendment; it is not a no-registration production path.

**Needs registration, with realistic sequencing:**
- *UIDAI Sub-AUA under an existing KUA* — a direct AUA/KUA licence is realistically reserved for large regulated entities; the fast, common path for a startup is a commercial Sub-AUA agreement under an already-licensed KUA (a bank, or a KYC vendor like Digio/Setu), which can be arranged in weeks to a few months once a partner is chosen.
- *Account Aggregator FIU status* — becoming a Financial Information User requires sectoral-regulator recognition (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA depending on the use case) plus Sahamati's technical certification and onboarding; Sahamati is the RBI-recognized SRO running the central registry/certification for the ecosystem and operates "Sahamati Labs" as an innovation arm for ecosystem builders ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/)) — budget several months for certification plus sandbox testing.
- *ABDM HIU/HIP* — relevant only once/if ApplyOnce builds the health vertical (hospital OPD autofill); requires sandbox registration and a Health ID linkage; treat as a later-stage, health-vertical-specific step.
- *DPDP Consent Manager registration* — formal registration with the Data Protection Board becomes available as the DPDP Rules 2025 are operationalized; worth pursuing once ApplyOnce has scale and can absorb the compliance overhead, since it converts today's "designed to DEPA pattern" posture into a certified status.

**A rough regulatory roadmap:** Start the DigiLocker Requester and UIDAI OVSE applications after entity setup while launching non-regulated hosted forms and user-controlled autofill. Pursue Sub-AUA, regulated FIU partnerships or ABDM only when a narrow lawful use case and commercial partner justify the certification work.

**Ecosystem support to lean on:** **iSPIRT** — the volunteer-driven foundation behind DEPA, OCEN and AA policy design, organized around policy, "playbooks," and market-catalyst working groups (community@ispirt.in) ([iSPIRT](https://ispirt.in/)) — its working groups are the fastest route to credibility and early visibility into how consent-manager rules get enforced. **Sahamati** for AA onboarding, sandbox and its Aikya/Labs programs. **NASSCOM** and **MeitY Startup Hub** for DPI policy advocacy and programs including **GENESIS** (deep-tech, Tier-2/3 startups) and **Samridh** (accelerator-style seed-funding access); **NIDHI** is a Department of Science & Technology umbrella of pre-seed/seed grants often bundled into the same conversation, worth applying to in parallel. Several state startup policies (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Gujarat, UP) add seed grants, GST reimbursement or co-working credits to extend runway.

## F. Risks and moats

**Chicken-and-egg.** Citizens won't build a verified profile until enough institutions accept it; institutions won't integrate until enough citizens already have profiles. The only proven way past this (Common App, Myinfo, Plaid) is to pick one high-frequency, low-competition wedge and force both sides to show up for it before broadening — see Phase 1 of the GTM plan.

**Government builds it themselves.** MeitY has, in four years, shipped DigiLocker, MeriPehchaan/NSSO, APAAR, and Entity Locker (as recently as January 2025), and keeps expanding Bhashini and the Account Aggregator network — an accelerating appetite to extend the "locker + SSO" pattern into new domains ([informatics.nic.in](https://informatics.nic.in/files/websites/april-2025/entity-locker-a-game-changer-in-business-document-management.php)). A MeitY-built "DigiLocker autofill" layer is a real existential risk. ApplyOnce's defensible posture: be **complementary infrastructure that consumes DigiLocker/AA/APAAR/ABDM as rails** and win on what government moves slowly on — the extension "recipe" library and a genuinely polished UX — rather than compete on the rails themselves.

**Trust/privacy backlash.** DigiYatra's documented "defective consent" controversy — coerced enrollment by airport staff, a Section-8 structure partly outside RTI scrutiny, and third-party SDK data-sharing concerns raised by the Internet Freedom Foundation ([TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/digi-yatra-and-the-defect-in-the-idea-of-consent/), [Panoptic](https://panoptic.in/case-study/the-dangers-of-digiyatra-facial-recognition-enabled-paperless-air-travel-saveourprivacy)) — shows Indian press and civil society are primed to scrutinize exactly this kind of platform. One dark-pattern consent flow or one breach and the brand is finished; every `SourceChip` and `consent_id` invariant in the product exists precisely to make this auditable rather than assumed.

**Aadhaar Act constraints.** Only licensed AUAs/KUAs (and their Sub-AUAs) can perform online Aadhaar authentication/e-KYC; a startup cannot legally pull Aadhaar data directly without going through one of these routes or relying on user-side offline XML — this legal boundary is what shapes the Day-1-vs-needs-registration split in Section E and cannot be shortcut.

**Data-breach liability.** The DPDP Act carries penalties up to ₹250 crore for breaches; a platform that aggregates identity + income + caste + health + family data in one place is an unusually attractive target. The mitigation is architectural: store facts and a consent ledger, not raw source documents, to minimize the blast radius of any single breach.

**Moats.** (1) **Consent ledger + structured facts** — a fine-grained, auditable record of what was shared, with whom, under which consent, independent of the underlying document; more portable across arbitrary forms than DigiLocker's document-sharing or AA's account-linking. (2) **Extension "recipes"** — a maintained field-mapping library for hundreds of legacy portals is laborious to replicate and compounds with usage (more users → more portal coverage → more useful to the next user). (3) **Institution network effects** — once an exam body or college integrates "Apply with ApplyOnce," switching cost is high, mirroring why Common App's college network (not its UI) is its real moat. (4) **Family graph** — a consented, PPP-style family linkage is powerful for dependent/co-applicant scholarships and income-linked benefits, and hard for point-solution KYC vendors to build since it needs durable, multi-person trust rather than a single transaction.

## G. Recommended 12-month GTM plan

**Phase 1 — Months 1–4, narrow wedge.** Pick one Tier-2 city with high coaching-institute density and strong CSC penetration (e.g., Kota, Patna, Indore or Lucknow) and one low-competition, high-repeat-application use case — **scholarships (NSP's ~2 crore students/₹8,000 crore/year base) or a state SSC/PSC-style recruitment exam** — rather than JEE/NEET, which are tightly controlled by NTA's own portal and less receptive to third-party overlays. Ship: DigiLocker requester integration, offline-Aadhaar-XML upload, and an extension MVP covering 5–10 of the highest-volume legacy portals (NSP plus one SSC/state portal). Partner with 2–3 coaching institutes as first distribution. **Metrics:** verified profiles created, successful autofills, time saved per application.

**Phase 2 — Months 5–8, institution pilot + CSC channel.** Sign 1–2 institutions or a scholarship/exam body for a live "Apply with ApplyOnce" button; onboard 20–50 CSC/VLE operators in one district as a paid channel who create ApplyOnce profiles as part of their existing certificate/form-filling business. Start the Sub-AUA/KUA partner conversation and begin the Sahamati AA-FIU certification process in parallel. **Metrics:** institution partners live, CSC-driven signups, consent events logged, re-use rate (applications per profile ≥2).

**Phase 3 — Months 9–12, scale and regulatory maturity.** Expand the extension recipe library to 50+ portals, add family-graph and income/caste-certificate facts, and pursue an Entity-Locker/API-Setu-style pilot MoU with one state government portal as public validation (with NASSCOM/iSPIRT endorsement if possible). Explore ABDM HIU for a hospital-OPD-registration vertical only if traction supports it. **Metrics:** MAU/verified-profile ratio, B2B revenue per verified application, CSC network size, and cost per application versus the manual/clerk baseline.

**The pitch, in one paragraph:** Every year, tens of millions of Indians re-type the same dozen facts about themselves — name, Aadhaar, category certificate, income, address, marks, bank details — into hundreds of different exam, college, scholarship, KYC and hospital forms, re-uploading the same documents and re-proving the same eligibility each time; Singapore solved this with Myinfo (200,000 prefilled transactions a day, 80% faster applications), and India has already built world-class rails to do the same — DigiLocker's 676 million users, the Account Aggregator's 2.88 billion linked accounts, APAAR's 263 million student IDs — but no one has built the *consumer-owned, consent-ledgered "verify once" layer* that sits on top and actually fills in the form. ApplyOnce is that layer: a verified citizen profile with one-tap consent, an "Apply with ApplyOnce" button for institutions, and a browser extension that autofills the legacy portals that will never get a native API — turning India's digital public infrastructure into the last-mile product experience it has been missing.

**Five investor objections, answered:**
1. *"DigiLocker/MeitY will just build this."* MeitY builds document lockers and SSO (DigiLocker, MeriPehchaan, Entity Locker) at multi-year cadence and has never shipped a third-party form-autofill layer; government rollout speed (the EU's multi-year EUDI struggle, Apple Wallet mDLs reaching only 15 US states after years) is structurally slower than a startup's iteration loop — ApplyOnce's job is the last mile, consuming rather than competing with government rails.
2. *"Chicken-and-egg — no one will build a profile with no institutions live."* Common App, Myinfo and Plaid all solved this by seeding one narrow, high-frequency, low-competition wedge (a scholarship program or one exam body, not JEE/NEET) before broadening — Phase 1 above is designed explicitly around that sequencing.
3. *"This is just another KYC vendor in a crowded field (Signzy/HyperVerge/IDfy/Setu)."* Those are point-verification APIs a business calls once per transaction with no persistent, consumer-owned, cross-institution profile; ApplyOnce is structurally closer to Plaid or Myinfo — a reusable layer the *user* owns and reuses across dozens of institutions — and can buy those vendors' APIs as suppliers rather than compete with them.
4. *"Privacy backlash risk (DigiYatra) kills trust."* Precisely because DigiYatra's failure was a *consent* failure, not a technology failure, ApplyOnce's architecture (SourceChip on every rendered fact, a `consent_id` on every share, DEPA-pattern consent ledger) is built to make consent auditable and revocable by design, converting the industry's biggest liability into a differentiator investors and regulators can verify.
5. *"How do you launch before regulated integrations?"* Launch hosted forms, partner APIs, manual non-Aadhaar evidence and user-controlled autofill first. Keep provider demonstrations synthetic until DigiLocker Requester, OVSE or other role-specific approvals are complete.
